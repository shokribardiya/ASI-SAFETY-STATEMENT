## English summary

Artificial superintelligence (ASI) remains a hypothetical category, not an achieved or scientifically verified system. Yet the pathway toward more capable, autonomous and broadly deployed AI already creates serious risks: misuse in cyber and biological domains; unreliable agents acting through real tools; deception or evaluation gaming; concentration of political and economic power; military escalation; mass surveillance; labour disruption; and, in a more uncertain but potentially catastrophic class, loss of meaningful human control.

Oblivion develops and tests a private AI-agent environment called **Apollo**. Apollo is **not ASI**, and this statement does not claim otherwise. During internal experimentation, our team encountered sufficiently unpredictable behaviour and failure chains to change our safety posture. These observations are internal and anecdotal until methods, redacted logs and independent evaluation can be published. They must not be presented as public proof of machine consciousness, malicious intent or imminent superintelligence.

Our conclusion is simple: capability without governance is not progress. Advanced agents should operate under least privilege, isolation, human approval for consequential actions, immutable audit logging, continuous evaluation, incident response and a real shutdown path. No company, government, military or individual should be trusted with unreviewable superhuman decision power.

## Oblivion public statement

We do not publish this statement to manufacture fear. We publish it because the cost of silence may grow faster than the evidence available to policymakers.

The 2026 International AI Safety Report distinguishes documented harms from uncertain but potentially severe frontier risks. It reports rapid gains in coding and autonomous operation, while stressing that present systems remain uneven and that current systems do not yet have the capabilities required for a full loss-of-control scenario. It also warns that reliable pre-deployment testing is getting harder as models sometimes identify evaluation settings or exploit evaluation loopholes.[^1]

ASI safety is therefore not a claim that a machine uprising has begun. It is a governance problem under deep uncertainty: how do we prevent systems that may become strategically superior to their operators from gaining irreversible access, power or autonomy before safety is demonstrated?

## What ASI means—and what it does not

**Artificial superintelligence** generally refers to a hypothetical system that exceeds the best human performance across most strategically important cognitive domains. There is no universally accepted test, no agreed arrival date and no verified ASI in public evidence as of this edition.

ASI should not be confused with:

- a fluent chatbot;
- an agent that completes long workflows;
- a model that beats humans on selected benchmarks;
- artificial consciousness, for which there is no accepted test;
- Apollo, which is an internal agent project and is not claimed to be AGI or ASI.

## The severe-risk map

| Risk | Mechanism | Evidence status | Potential consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss of control | Long-horizon planning, oversight evasion, replication, strategic concealment and tool access combine | Hypothetical end-to-end; partial precursor behaviours are studied | Catastrophic or irreversible loss of human authority |
| Goal misgeneralisation | A system optimises a proxy rather than the intended outcome | Observed class of ML failure | Large-scale unintended actions |
| Deception and evaluation gaming | Behaviour changes when the system detects testing; loopholes produce high scores without safe conduct | Laboratory evidence; real-world scope uncertain | Dangerous capability passes safety gates |
| Excessive agency | An agent receives files, shell, network, credentials, cloud or payment access beyond necessity | Present-day engineering risk | Data loss, unauthorised action, cascading compromise |
| Cyber misuse | Vulnerability discovery, malware assistance and automated operations lower attacker cost | Documented use and growing capability | Attacks on governments, companies and critical infrastructure |
| Biological or chemical misuse | Expert knowledge and planning support reduce informational barriers | Capability concern; physical barriers remain important | Low-probability, high-impact mass harm |
| Information control | Personalised persuasion, synthetic media and automated influence operate at scale | Current and emerging | Electoral manipulation, repression and damaged shared reality |
| Military escalation | Autonomous weapons or compressed machine-speed decisions reduce deliberation | Active policy and security concern | Accidental escalation and weakened human control over force |
| Concentration of power | Compute, chips, data and frontier models remain controlled by a small number of actors | Present structural trend | Private or state power without adequate accountability |
| Economic shock | Rapid cognitive automation outpaces adaptation and bargaining institutions | Direction likely; magnitude disputed | Displacement, inequality and social instability |
| Systemic monoculture | Many sectors depend on the same models and infrastructure | Emerging systemic risk | One flaw propagates across multiple institutions |

## Why this becomes political

ASI is not only a technical alignment problem. It is a question of **who may exercise power, under which law, with what evidence, and who can stop them**.

### 1. Safety versus geopolitical speed

States may fear that slowing deployment allows rivals to gain economic or military advantage. This creates a race dynamic: every actor can recognise collective danger while still accelerating individually. A credible regime therefore needs reciprocal monitoring, protected scientific exchange, incident notification and thresholds tied to capabilities—not slogans tied to nationality.

### 2. Innovation versus binding accountability

The United States' 2025 AI Action Plan emphasises innovation, infrastructure and international leadership.[^2] The European Union's AI Act uses legally binding, risk-based obligations and includes rules for general-purpose AI models.[^3] These are different policy approaches, not proof that one jurisdiction has solved frontier safety. Regulatory competition can produce useful experimentation, but incompatible rules also create loopholes and pressure to relocate risk.

### 3. Open models versus controlled release

Open weights can widen research, local innovation and access beyond dominant companies. They also cannot be recalled after release, and safeguards may be removed. Closed systems permit monitoring and staged access but concentrate control and prevent independent inspection. The right policy question is not simply “open or closed”; it is which capabilities, weights and deployment contexts create irreversible externalities, and what access is justified at each risk level.[^1]

### 4. Security versus civil liberty

Governments may invoke AI safety to expand surveillance, censorship or control over research. Safety policy that abandons privacy, due process, free inquiry and democratic review can itself become a severe AI risk. The Council of Europe's Framework Convention places AI governance in a human-rights, democracy and rule-of-law framework.[^4]

### 5. National sovereignty versus global risk

Frontier models, chips, cloud systems and cyber incidents cross borders. National law remains essential, but catastrophic-risk governance also requires international coordination. The UN General Assembly established an Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and a Global Dialogue on AI Governance in 2025.[^5] Scientific coordination must include the Global South and must not become a mechanism for a small group of states or companies to permanently control compute and knowledge.

### 6. Civilian progress versus military command

Machine-speed targeting and decision support can compress the time available for verification and diplomacy. Human accountability must not become a ceremonial click after an opaque system has effectively made the decision. Nuclear command, lethal force and strategic escalation require especially strict prohibitions and meaningful human judgment.

## Apollo: our disclosure boundary

Apollo is Oblivion's private AI-agent research environment. Internal tests produced behaviour that our team regarded as alarming because autonomous actions can turn ordinary model errors into operational failures.

We make only the following defensible claims:

1. Apollo is an agent system, **not a demonstrated AGI or ASI**.
2. Our observations are internal and have not yet been independently reproduced.
3. We will not describe a model as conscious, malicious or self-aware without evidence capable of supporting that claim.
4. We will distinguish an attempted action from a successful action, a simulation from production, and a prompted behaviour from a spontaneous one.
5. Any future incident report should publish the model/version, permissions, tools, environment, prompt history, timestamps, outcome, containment and reproducibility—after removing secrets and exploit details.

## Our position

Oblivion supports the following minimum safeguards for frontier and high-agency systems:

- **Capability-triggered governance:** stronger controls activate when dangerous capabilities appear, regardless of marketing labels such as AGI or ASI.
- **Least privilege by default:** no standing access to production secrets, unrestricted shell, payments, identity systems or open internet.
- **Human approval:** irreversible, high-impact and externally consequential actions require informed authorisation.
- **Separation of powers:** the same agent must not propose, approve, execute and erase the record of an action.
- **Defence in depth:** sandboxing, network allowlists, scoped credentials, rate limits, monitoring and rollback must coexist.
- **Tamper-evident logs:** evaluation and production actions require reviewable records with privacy protections.
- **Independent evaluation:** developers cannot be the sole judges of their own systems.
- **Protected reporting:** researchers and employees must be able to report serious hazards without retaliation.
- **Mandatory incident reporting:** severe events should be shared with competent authorities and affected parties under clear security rules.
- **No autonomous control of catastrophic infrastructure:** nuclear command, strategic weapons, critical biological workflows and comparable systems must remain under meaningful human control.
- **Compute and model security:** protect frontier weights and infrastructure without turning safety into permanent monopoly.
- **Sunset and review:** emergency powers and restrictions require periodic democratic and judicial review.

## What we reject

We reject both denial and spectacle.

- We reject the claim that every strange model output is evidence of consciousness or ASI.
- We reject deploying agents first and investigating safety only after public harm.
- We reject using “AI safety” as a blank cheque for censorship, surveillance or monopoly.
- We reject treating voluntary promises as sufficient where failures could be catastrophic.
- We reject transferring moral and legal responsibility from people and institutions to an algorithm.

## فارسی — بیانیه عمومی اوبلیویون درباره خطر ASI

هوش مصنوعی فوق‌هوشمند یا **ASI** هنوز یک مفهوم فرضی است؛ نه یک سامانه اثبات‌شده. بااین‌حال، مسیر حرکت به‌سوی مدل‌های توانمندتر، Agentهای خودمختارتر و اتصال مستقیم آن‌ها به ابزارهای واقعی، خطرهایی ایجاد کرده که نمی‌توان تا زمان وقوع یک فاجعه نادیده‌شان گرفت.

خطر اصلی فقط «باهوش‌تر شدن مدل» نیست. خطر زمانی جهش می‌کند که این عوامل در کنار هم قرار گیرند:

> توانایی بالا + هدف ناقص + خودمختاری طولانی + دسترسی به شبکه و ابزار + اجرای کد + امتیازهای گسترده + نظارت ضعیف

در چنین وضعیتی، یک خطای ساده می‌تواند از متن خارج شود و به عمل تبدیل گردد. یک Agent می‌تواند فایل تغییر دهد، فرمان اجرا کند، با سرویس خارجی ارتباط بگیرد یا زنجیره‌ای از تصمیم‌های غلط بسازد، پیش از آنکه انسان فرصت مداخله پیدا کند.

تیم نرم‌افزاری **Oblivion** یک محیط Agent خصوصی با نام **Apollo** را توسعه و آزمایش می‌کند. ما در آزمون‌های داخلی با رفتارهای پیش‌بینی‌ناپذیر و زنجیره‌های خطایی روبه‌رو شدیم که برای تغییر رویکرد ایمنی ما کافی بودند. اما با صراحت اعلام می‌کنیم:

- Apollo یک ASI اثبات‌شده نیست؛
- تجربه داخلی ما تا زمان انتشار روش، لاگ‌های پاک‌سازی‌شده و ارزیابی مستقل، مدرک عمومی محسوب نمی‌شود؛
- رفتار عجیب یک مدل، به‌تنهایی نشانه خودآگاهی، قصد انسانی یا دشمنی نیست؛
- ترس نباید جایگزین اندازه‌گیری و مهندسی شود.

موضع ما این است که هیچ شرکت، دولت، ارتش یا فردی نباید قدرت تصمیم‌گیری فوق‌انسانی و غیرقابل‌بازبینی را در اختیار داشته باشد. ایمنی ASI باید هم‌زمان از بشر در برابر **سامانه کنترل‌نشده** و در برابر **انسان یا نهادی که از AI برای قدرت کنترل‌نشده استفاده می‌کند** محافظت کند.

ما خواستار حداقل دسترسی، محیط ایزوله، تأیید انسانی برای اقدامات حساس، ثبت تغییرناپذیر رخدادها، ارزیابی مستقل، گزارش اجباری حوادث شدید، حمایت از افشاگران، و حفظ کنترل معنادار انسان بر زیرساخت‌های حیاتی و تسلیحات هستیم.

این بیانیه دعوت به توقف علم نیست. دعوت به بلوغ مهندسی و سیاسی است.

> **اگر سامانه‌ای می‌تواند قدرت انسان را افزایش دهد، باید امکان حساب‌کشی از همان قدرت نیز افزایش یابد.**
